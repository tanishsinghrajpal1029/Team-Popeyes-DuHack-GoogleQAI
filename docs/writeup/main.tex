  {\Large\bfseries Preparing Dicke States on Willow: the Mathematical Choices}\\[3pt]
  Team Popeyes $\cdot$ DuQuantum $\times$ Google Quantum AI Challenge, Standard Track $\cdot$ October 25, 2026

Our \file{create\_dicke\_state} returns an exact circuit of \CZ{} and \texttt{PhasedXZ} gates on
calibrated Willow qubits. This appendix states the mathematical facts that fixed each design
choice, each with a short justification. Every number is \emph{simulated}: calibrated
depolarizing gate errors, $T_1=68\,\mu$s~\cite{willow}, $T_\varphi=100\,\mu$s (assumed), and
asymmetric readout flips. Nothing here has run on hardware. File paths refer to the repository.

\section{Notation and the judges' score}

$\ket{\D Nn}=\binom Nn^{-1/2}\sum_{|b|=n}\ket b$, $K=\binom Nn$, $k=\min(n,N-n)$,
$R_y(\theta)=e^{-i\theta Y/2}$. Each case is judged from $10^4$ shots in the $Z$ basis and
$10^4$ in the $X$ basis:
\[
  S=\frac{1}{z+x},\qquad
  z=\frac{\chi^2}{Kf},\qquad
  \chi^2=\sum_{|b|=n}\frac{(c_b-F/K)^2}{F/K},\qquad
  x=\sum_{j=1}^{M}\epsilon_j^2 .
\]
Here $f$ is the fraction of $Z$ shots with exactly $n$ ones, $F=10^4f$, and $c_b$ counts valid
string $b$. $\epsilon_j$ is the relative error (absolute where the ideal is 0) of $\bar C_j$, the
average of $\langle\prod_{i\in A}X_i\rangle$ over all $j$-subsets $A$. $M$ is undisclosed; we
report $M=N$, the strictest choice.

\textbf{Ideal correlators.} $X_A=\prod_{i\in A}X_i$ flips the bits in $A$, so $\bra D X_A\ket D$
is the fraction of weight-$n$ strings that keep weight $n$, i.e.\ that have exactly $j/2$ ones in $A$:
$C^\star_j=\binom{j}{j/2}\binom{N-j}{n-j/2}/\binom Nn$ for even $j$, and $0$ for odd $j$.

\textbf{Fast scoring.} For one $X$-basis shot $b$ of weight $w$, the average of
$(-1)^{|b\cap A|}$ over all $j$-subsets $A$ is $\mathcal K_j(w)/\binom Nj$, with the Krawtchouk
polynomial $\mathcal K_j(w)=\sum_i(-1)^i\binom wi\binom{N-w}{j-i}$. So $x$ depends only on the
Hamming-weight histogram of the $X$ shots. That is what makes 20{,}000 simulated judged runs
per case cheap (\file{experiments/scoring.py}).

\section{The score is heavy-tailed: small $K$ and uniformity come first}\label{sec:score}

\textbf{Perfect state.} With no imbalance, $\chi^2\sim\chi^2_{K-1}$. Writing $a=Kfx$,
\[
  \E[S]=Kf\,\E\!\left[\frac{1}{\chi^2_{K-1}+a}\right]\approx
  \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{Kf}{K-3}, & K>3\quad\bigl(\E[1/\chi^2_\nu]=1/(\nu-2)\bigr),\\[8pt]
    \tfrac32 f\,e^{a/2}E_1(a/2)\;\approx\;\tfrac32 f\bigl(\ln\tfrac2a-\gamma\bigr), & K=3,\\[6pt]
    2f\sqrt{\pi/(2a)}\;\operatorname{erfcx}\!\sqrt{a/2}\;\approx\;\sqrt{\pi f/x}, & K=2.
  \end{cases}
\]
The $K=2$ and $K=3$ forms are exact, because the $\chi^2_1$ and $\chi^2_2$ densities integrate in
closed form. For $K>3$ the mean is finite even at $x=0$. For $K\le3$ only the $X$-term floor keeps
it finite, and at $K=2$ it grows like $x^{-1/2}$.

\textbf{Imbalance.} If valid string $b$ has probability $(1+\delta_b)/K$ inside the subspace,
$\chi^2$ becomes noncentral with
\[
  \lambda=\sum_b\frac{\bigl(F(1+\delta_b)/K-F/K\bigr)^2}{F/K}=F\,\overline{\delta^2}=10^4 f\,\overline{\delta^2}.
\]
Then $\E[\chi^2]=K-1+\lambda$, so $z$ grows by about $10^4\,\overline{\delta^2}/K$. A 1\% rms
imbalance lowers $\E[S]$ at $K=2$ from 51.4 to 33.3, and 3\% lowers it to 1.3. At $K=4$ the same
steps give 3.34, 2.53 and 0.53.

\textbf{Consequences.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item The judges sum single draws, so our objective is $\sum\E[S]$. We compute it by quadrature
        over the noncentral $\chi^2$ density, with $x$ held at its mean. A resampled mean is
        unusable at $K\le3$, where a few lucky draws dominate it.
  \item Priority order: a valid circuit on every case, since any valid circuit scores $>0$; then
        uniformity ($\delta_{\rm rms}\lesssim1\%$) and a low $x$ floor at small $K$; then
        leakage, which costs only $1/f$; then coherence at large $N$.
  \item Pre-compensating known biases is forbidden, because the noiseless state must be exact.
        Uniformity can only come from the construction and the choice of qubits.
\end{itemize}

\section{Two free reductions}

\textbf{Complement.} $X^{\otimes N}\ket{\D Nn}=\ket{\D N{N-n}}$, because flipping every bit maps
weight-$n$ strings bijectively onto weight-$(N-n)$ strings. We only build $k\le N/2$; the final
$X$ layer merges into the last single-qubit gates.

\textbf{Permutation symmetry.} The target is invariant under qubit permutations and the returned
qubits are a set. Routing permutations therefore never need undoing, and any placement of a
construction's shape on the chip is a valid answer.

\section{W states ($k=1$): a broadcast tree with $2N-3$ CZ}

\textbf{Construction.} Take a spanning tree of the chosen qubits with root $r$, and let $s(v)$ be
the size of the subtree under $v$. The root starts with the excitation. In each round, every qubit
$v$ that holds probability $m_v/N$ hands one child $c$ the fraction $p=s(c)/m_v$:
\[
  \ket1_v\ket0_c\mapsto\sqrt{1-p}\,\ket{10}+\sqrt p\,\ket{01},\qquad \ket{00}\mapsto\ket{00}.
\]
Afterwards $c$ holds $s(c)/N$ and $v$ keeps $(m_v-s(c))/N$. By induction every qubit ends with
exactly $1/N$. Every branch carries one excitation and all amplitudes are real and positive, so
the output is exactly $\ket{W_N}$.

\textbf{Cost.} The first transfer acts on a product input, so it is $R_y$ on $c$, a CNOT and an
$X$: 1 CZ. Every later transfer must handle both $\ket{00}$ and $\ket{10}$, because $v$ holds the
excitation only in some branches. That is a Givens rotation, 2 CZ. One CZ cannot do it: in
$(A_1\otimes B_1)\,\CZ\,(A_0\otimes B_0)\ket{x}\ket0$, keeping $\ket{00}$ a product state forces
$A_0$ to map basis states to basis states, and then $\ket{10}$ stays a product too (Bryan's
argument, \file{docs/bryan\_constructions.md}). The total is $1+2(N-2)=2N-3$ CZ. Exhaustive search
shows this is optimal for $W_3$ (3 CZ) and $W_4$ (5 CZ).

\textbf{Why a tree.} A chain has the same CZ count but $N-1$ sequential transfers. The tree runs
the transfers of a round in parallel: $W_{10}$ takes 15 moments instead of 37, and $x$ drops from
0.0075 to 0.0042 under idle noise. The circuit preserves excitation number, so every branch has
the same $T_1$ exposure and idle decay causes no imbalance.

\textbf{$K\le3$.} For $W_2$ the score is set by $x$ and $\lambda$, not by CZ count. We simulate
every candidate qubit pair under the calibrated noise and keep the one with the highest predicted
$\E[S]$: $+9\%$ expected score, $-5\%$ median, a deliberate trade toward the objective.

\section{Whole-state synthesis for $N\le8$}\label{sec:synth}

\textbf{Ansatz.} $L+1$ layers of $R_y$ on every qubit, separated by $L$ CZ layers drawn from a fixed
shape: a path or a $2\times m$ ladder, edge-coloured into brickwork matchings. Dicke amplitudes are
real and $\{R_y,\CZ\}$ circuits are real, so one angle per qubit per layer is all the freedom
needed. The restriction costs no generality: $ZR_y(\theta)Z=R_y(-\theta)$ is the same identity as
$XR_y(\theta)X=R_y(-\theta)$, on which the uniformly controlled rotations of~\cite{mottonen05} rely.
That construction therefore compiles to $R_y$ and CZ and, given enough connectivity, reaches
every real state.

\textbf{Optimization.} We minimize $1-\langle D|\psi(\theta)\rangle^2$ with L-BFGS, using an exact
gradient by backpropagation ($\partial_\theta R_y(\theta)=\tfrac12R_y(\theta+\pi)$), until the
infidelity is below $10^{-13}$. We start at the smallest brickwork depth that becomes exact. Then
we delete single CZs greedily, re-solve from the previous angles, and keep a deletion only if the
circuit is exact again (\file{experiments/synth.py}).

With one CZ fewer than deployed, the best infidelity is $3\times10^{-3}$ or worse for $\D42$,
$\D52$, $\D62$ and $\D63$ (\file{vqe\_gap.csv}), so approximate circuits are not an option.
Synthesis takes minutes, so the angles are cached in \file{main.py}. Placement (8 grid symmetries $\times$ translations) happens at
build time.

\textbf{Choosing among exact angle sets.} One CZ pattern admits many exact angle sets, and they
differ under coherent errors that our noise model lacks. Insert a small error $e^{-i\epsilon G}$
into a real circuit. For $R_y$ over-rotation $G\propto Y$, and $-iY$ is real, so the real
amplitudes change at first order and populations move by $O(\epsilon)$. For stray $Z$ phases and
CZ conditional-phase errors $G$ is real and diagonal. The first-order change is then $i$ times a
real vector, orthogonal to the amplitudes, so populations move only by $O(\epsilon^2)$. On the
deployed $\D42$, shrinking $\epsilon$ from $10^{-2}$ to $10^{-3}$ cuts the population error
$10\times$ for over-rotation and $100\times$ for stray $Z$. The pick therefore models only
over-rotation. With $L(c,\sigma)=\E[\mathrm{loss}]_c+\lambda_c(\sigma/1\%)^2/(Kf)$ and
$\sigma\in\{0,0.1,0.2,0.3,0.5,1\}\%$, we deploy the minimax-regret candidate
\[
  c^\star=\arg\min_c\,\max_\sigma\Bigl[L(c,\sigma)-\min_d L(d,\sigma)\Bigr],
\]
chosen on calibration seed 0 and checked on seeds 1--2. Sensitivity to 1\% over-rotation drops
1.1--5.5$\times$ ($\D52$: $\lambda$ from 50 to 9). Under our standard noise model, without
over-rotation, the change in ceiling share is between $-0.9$ and $+2.6$ points.
The 31-CZ $\D83$ has no alternative exact angle set yet.

\section{B\"artschi--Eidenbenz with synthesized SCS blocks}

B-E~\cite{be19} uses $\ket{\D mr}=\sqrt{r/m}\,\ket{\D{m-1}{r-1}}\ket1+\sqrt{(m-r)/m}\,\ket{\D{m-1}r}\ket0$
to reach $\ket{\D Nk}$ from $\ket{0^{N-k}1^k}$ by applying $\mathrm{SCS}_N,\mathrm{SCS}_{N-1},\dots,\mathrm{SCS}_2$.
$\mathrm{SCS}_m$ touches only the window of positions $m-k,\dots,m$.

\textbf{Observation.} Before step $m$ the first $m$ positions hold a sorted string, so the window
($w=k+1$ qubits) only ever sees inputs $\ket{0^{w-l}1^l}$ with $l=0,\dots,k$, plus $\ket{1^w}$ when
the window is the whole register. On these inputs the block must act as
\[
  \ket{0^{w-l}1^l}\mapsto\sqrt{l/m}\,\ket{0^{w-l}1^l}+\sqrt{(m-l)/m}\,\ket{0^{w-l-1}1^l0}
  \quad(1\le l\le k,\ l<m),
\]
and fix $l=0$ and $l=m$. So each block is an isometry on a $(k+1)$- or $(k+2)$-dimensional subspace,
not a full unitary on $2^{k+1}$ dimensions. The rest of the unitary is free.

\textbf{Synthesis.} We fit each block on $k+1$ consecutive path qubits with the same ansatz and
pruning, using the loss $1-\bigl(\tfrac1B\sum_b\bra{\mathrm{out}_b}U\ket{\mathrm{in}_b}\bigr)^2$.
It vanishes only when every column is right \emph{with a common sign}, so superpositions of inputs
are mapped coherently. The CZ pattern is pruned once per $k$, and the angles are refit for every
$m$. The whole B-E circuit is then nearest-neighbour on a path and needs no routing. Inner blocks
cost 2, 4, 12, 17 and 29 CZ for $k=1,\dots,5$, so $\D N2$ costs about $4N$ CZ
(\file{experiments/scs\_synth.py}). On the hardware track these circuits are a fallback; they are
the main path for $N>20$.

\section{Placement: the cost is $\log\E[z]$}

\textbf{Derivation.} From Section~\ref{sec:score}, $\E[z]\approx(K-1+\lambda)/(Kf)$, so
$\log\E[z]\approx\mathrm{const}-\log f+\log\bigl(1+\lambda/(K-1)\bigr)$. Approximating $f$ by the
probability that no error event occurs gives $-\log f\approx\sum e$ over all error locations. A
placement's cost is therefore
\[
  C=\sum_{(q,q')}u_{qq'}\,e^{\CZ}_{qq'}+\sum_q\Bigl[g_q\,e^{1q}_q+\bigl(1-\tfrac nN\bigr)e^{0\to1}_q+\tfrac nN\,e^{1\to0}_q\Bigr]
  +\tau\Bigl(\tfrac1{T_1}+\tfrac1{T_\varphi}\Bigr)+\log\Bigl(1+\frac{\lambda_{\rm ro}}{K-1}\Bigr),
\]
with $u_{qq'}$ CZ uses of coupler $(q,q')$, $g_q$ single-qubit gates per qubit, and $\tau$ the idle time.
Each qubit of $\ket{\D Nn}$ reads 1 with probability $n/N$, and $1\to0$ readout errors are about
twice the $0\to1$ rate, which is why the readout term is weighted.

\textbf{Readout imbalance.} A valid string $b$ survives readout with probability
$\prod_{i\in b}(1-e^{1\to0}_i)\prod_{i\notin b}(1-e^{0\to1}_i)\approx c\,\exp\bigl(-\sum_{i\in b}d_i\bigr)$,
with $c$ independent of $b$ and $d_i=e^{1\to0}_i-e^{0\to1}_i$. Hence $\delta_b\approx-\sum_{i\in b}(d_i-\bar d)$. Its variance
over random $n$-subsets is $\tfrac{n(N-n)}{N-1}\mathrm{Var}(d)$, so
\[
  \lambda_{\rm ro}=10^4 f\,\frac{n(N-n)}{N-1}\,\mathrm{Var}(d)
\]
(\file{main.\_readout\_lambda}). A random layout gives $\delta_{\rm rms}\approx0.6\%$. Since amplitudes
cannot be pre-compensated, qubit choice is the only remedy.

\textbf{Winner's curse.} The minimum of many noisy estimates is biased low, so every calibrated
rate enters as value $+1\sigma$. This changed none of 75 placements on the notebook's
uniform-uncertainty calibrations; it matters only when uncertainties differ between qubits.

\section{Compilation}

Single-qubit runs between CZs merge into one \texttt{PhasedXZ}, and every moment is all-CZ or
all-single-qubit. Dynamical decoupling inserts $XYXY=-I$, which leaves the noiseless state unchanged
up to a global phase. It is applied only after a qubit's first gate, since $\ket0$ cannot dephase,
and only in idle windows with at least 4 free single-qubit slots: short idles have nothing to
refocus, so there only the pulse error shows.

\section{Portfolio with Bryan's solution}

Bryan's independent solution is vendored in \file{main.py}; its derivations are in
\file{docs/bryan\_constructions.md}. It rests on a 1-CZ controlled $R_y$ onto a target known to be
$\ket0$ ($R_y(\tfrac\theta2)ZR_y(-\tfrac\theta2)=R_y(\theta)Z$ and $Z\ket0=\ket0$), a 5-CZ
``moving-hole'' gate and a 4-CZ merged top block that make each SCS step nearest-neighbour, and a
two-arm split
$\ket{\D Nk}=\sum_j\sqrt{P(j)}\,\ket{\D a{k-j}}\ket{\D bj}$ with hypergeometric
$P(j)=\binom bj\binom a{k-j}/\binom Nk$ (compare the divide-and-conquer circuits of~\cite{be22}).
$P(j)$ is written in unary by a $(k-1)$-CZ thermometer chain and copied across a ladder with $k$ CZ
in one layer, after which both arms run B-E in parallel. Under our harness it wins for $k=2$ at
$N\ge6$ (1--7\%) and for $k\ge3$ at $N\ge9$ (21--39\%; $\D{10}5$ needs 85 CZ against our 185), and
\file{main.py} dispatches by that measured rule.

\section{Bonus track: depth only}\label{sec:bonus}

The bonus scores $\sum_{N\le15,\,n\le N/2} Nn/\text{moments}$ with no noise. The organizers'
first-round feedback put our entry at 37.15 against a leader at 41.10; all 56 cases were exact,
so the gap was depth. Three facts fixed the design. (i) \texttt{Willow105.validate\_circuit}
rejects classically controlled operations, so a feedforward circuit would fail the validator the
judges are told to use: the entry is unitary. (ii) A split circuit has $2\cdot(\text{CZ depth})+1$
moments and the CZ depth is set by the B-E sweep: each arm position costs about 18 moments at
$k\ge5$ (10 at $k=3$, 5 at $k=2$), and the sweeps for the last few positions of an arm run on the
same qubits with nothing in parallel. (iii) Those sweeps implement a small Dicke unitary,
$U_{m_0}\colon\ket{0^{m_0-r}1^r}\mapsto\ket{\D{m_0}r}$ for $r\le\min(k,m_0)$, an isometry we can
synthesize exactly with the batch fitter of Section~\ref{sec:synth}, fewest layers first
(\file{experiments/tail\_synth.py}). Replacing the whole arm by one block when a block exists
($U_7$ with $r\le2$: 27 moments against a 37-moment sweep arm; $U_6$ with $r\le4$: 43 against 97)
and the last 4--7 positions otherwise, choosing the arm that carries the thermometer, and porting
a teammate's staggered W tree (one to two moments shallower at $N\ge5$) gives
\textbf{43.79}. Per case: $\D{12}4$ 88 $\to$ 51, $\D{14}4$ 106 $\to$ 63, $\D{13}2$ 40 $\to$ 31, $\D{15}2$ 46 $\to$ 35,
$\D{15}7$ 160 $\to$ 144, $W_9$ 14 $\to$ 12. What remains is $k\ge5$ (12 cases, 8.5 points), whose
arms carry every weight up to the arm length; no exact block beyond $U_4$ with $r\le4$ was found
for them. Synthesis from scratch (Givens-only, 2D patches, complex single-qubit layers) and
divide-and-conquer beyond two arms were also tried and did not help (\file{ARCHITECTURE.md},
Sections 5--6).

\section{What did not work}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Whole-state synthesis at $N=9$--$10$.} An overnight run found exact circuits of
        33, 35, 98 and 136 CZ for $\D92$, $\D{10}2$, $\D94$ and $\D93$, against 27, 31, 63 and 50
        deployed. Random-start brickwork stops scaling at $N\approx9$.
  \item \textbf{Compressing Bryan's circuits.} Re-parametrizing his $\D93$ and deleting single CZs
        recovered exactness 0 of 8 times (best residual $1.6\times10^{-4}$).
  \item \textbf{ALAP scheduling} to keep qubits in $\ket0$ longer changed $\E[S]$ by $-1.4\%$ to
        $+0.1\%$, so it was removed.
\end{itemize}
